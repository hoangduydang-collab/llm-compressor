Phala's AWQ-produced W4AFP8 is clean — the M3 AWQ pathology does not replicate

A three-arm paired evaluation of the community PhalaCloud W4AFP8 checkpoint (weights chosen by AWQ) and the official FP8 release against the official BF16 baseline. Every W4AFP8 task lands within the statistical noise band of BF16, and — unlike MiniMax-M3's AWQ — it spends fewer reasoning tokens than the baseline on the hard tasks. The M3 non-termination failure is a model×recipe interaction, not a property of AWQ.

Pipeline: benchmarks quality.orchestrator (standalone arms) + quality.rebuild_delta Run: glm52full7 (07-22 → 07-23) Engine: SGLang 0.5.13.post1 · lm-eval 0.4.10 Baseline: zai-org/GLM-5.2 BF16 (TP32, 4×8 H100)

Bottom line

Read this if you have thirty seconds

The question behind this run: MiniMax-M3's AWQ quants showed a severe, specific failure — runaway thinking loops that exhaust the generation budget, tanking accuracy and inflating token spend. Does the same thing happen to another AWQ-produced checkpoint on another model? No. On GLM-5.2, the PhalaCloud W4AFP8 (int4 experts chosen by AWQ, FP8 activations) recovers 96.2–102.4% of BF16 across all seven tasks — every delta inside the 2σ noise band — and its answer flips are symmetric churn (GPQA: 15 lost / 17 gained, vs M3-AWQ's 53 / 5). On token spend it is the anti-M3: it uses 14% fewer tokens than BF16 on GPQA and 11% fewer on IFEval, and exhausts the 64k budget less often than the baseline does.

The official FP8 release is equally healthy, with two footnotes: a marginal GSM8K dip (−1.4 pts, just past the 2σ band — one excursion among 14 comparisons) and an above-noise MMLU gain (+2.4 pts). On the quality axis, both quants are ship-grade; serving performance was not measured in this run.

Method

What was measured, and what the numbers do and don't mean

Three arms, each self-hosted on SGLang 0.5.13.post1 and evaluated with the same standalone general-suite protocol (the sequential-arm variant of the M3 pipeline; deltas rebuilt offline with quality.rebuild_delta, 2σ noise-floor bands from the baseline's own sampling stderr):

  • BF16 baseline — zai-org/GLM-5.2 (753B, 1.4 TB), 4 nodes, TP32.
  • FP8 — zai-org/GLM-5.2-FP8 (official release, 704 GB), 2 nodes, TP16.
  • W4AFP8 — PhalaCloud/GLM-5.2-W4AFP8 (407 GB): routed-expert weights int4 group-128 chosen by AWQ, static-scale FP8 activations; attention/shared-experts FP8; 1 node, TP8. SGLang's w4afp8 path, per the checkpoint's own README.

Seven tasks — GSM8K (5-shot), IFEval, GPQA-Diamond CoT (0-shot), MMLU (5-shot), ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, TruthfulQA-mc2 — matching the M3 full4 suite. Decoding is greedy (temperature 0), the suite's reproducibility convention; thinking is on (the glm45 parser strips reasoning and scores only the final answer, so a budget-exhausted, answer-less response scores zero). Generation budget is 65,536 tokens — the M3 tok64k depth — with per-request token accounting (the usage proxy) on every generative task. Serving config held identical across arms: FP8 e4m3 KV cache, 131k context, chunked prefill 2048, same kernels/env.

Comparability caveat

These scores are valid for quant-vs-quant and quant-vs-BF16 decisions — all arms run the identical protocol. They are not comparable to public leaderboards or to Phala's own model-card table (which used temperature 1.0 / top_p 0.95 on H200s): we decode greedily, serve with an FP8 KV cache on all arms (including the BF16 baseline — uniform, so deltas isolate weight quantization), and ~20% of GPQA items exhaust even a 64k budget under greedy CoT on every arm, depressing absolute GPQA scores equally. MC tasks run as loglikelihood scoring with a chat template — absolute MMLU/ARC numbers are low by public-recipe standards for all arms alike.

What this establishes

The M3 failure is not "AWQ breaks reasoning models." An AWQ-produced 4-bit expert quant of a 753B reasoning MoE passes every probe that M3's AWQ failed — on a harder budget test than M3's original run. The non-termination loop is a model×recipe interaction (M3-specific sensitivity, possibly to attention/expert quantization choices or calibration), which sharpens the M3 follow-up: the question is what M3's AWQ recipe damaged, not whether AWQ is usable. It also means our own in-house AWQ→W4AFP8 recipes have an existence proof to aim at on GLM-class models.

Differences to keep in mind

This is one checkpoint, not a controlled recipe study: Phala's AWQ calibration set/recipe differ from our M3 r5; GLM-5.2's W4AFP8 keeps attention and shared experts in FP8 (only routed experts are int4), while M3 recipes quantized more; engines differ (SGLang vs vLLM); and GLM-5.2 vs M3 are different bases. The clean result bounds "AWQ is inherently broken" out; it does not localize M3's failure.
