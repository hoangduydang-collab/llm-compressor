W4AFP8 decodes faster than every M3 provider but one — on half the hardware

Our in-house W4AFP8 quant serves at 137 tok/s single-user (second only to Nebius among commercial M3 endpoints), 3.8× BF16's per-GPU reasoning throughput, and holds a 1-second agentic p95 TTFT SLO to concurrency 16 — same checkpoint we shipped, zero requantization. Results are organized on two axes: which kernel (model fixed) and which quantization (kernel fixed). Quality verdicts: M3_OFFICIAL_QUALITY_RESULTS.html.

Bottom line

Read this if you have thirty seconds

In-house W4AFP8 on one 8×H100 node, stock vLLM plus our patched Humming MoE kernel: 137 tok/s single-user decode (BF16: 81 on two nodes), 3,267 tok/s aggregate at concurrency 64 (BF16: 1,700 on twice the GPUs), a 1-second agentic p95 TTFT SLO held to concurrency 16, and 131 tok/s decode with a 100k-token prompt — the full serve-ready vs BF16 comparison is one table. The speed ladder is set by format and kernel, not quantization algorithm: GPTQ and AWQ on the same kernel are identical in every cell; the community W4A16 and vendor MXFP8 checkpoints trail at every operating point.

Method

Two measurement paths, two comparison axes

aiperf 0.8.0 drives synthetic traffic against each arm's OpenAI endpoint; engine held fixed (vLLM 0.24.0, identical flags, FP8 KV cache, 131,072-token context). Quant arms: one 8×H100 node (TP8). BF16: two nodes (TP16/ray). Each axis reports both paths:

  • Primary path (suite-native) — reasoning: 1k input, 8k forced decode (ignore_eos; isolates kernel speed from model verbosity), temp 0.6, thinking on, concurrency 1/4/16/64; agentic: multi-turn, M3-calibrated tau2 shape (7,300-token cacheable prefix, ~100 output tokens/turn), warm (KV reuse) vs cold (cache-defeated), concurrency 1/4/16/32.
  • AA-style sweep — Artificial-Analysis axes: input 1k/10k/100k × concurrency 1/10, natural output length (AA's answer-token floors recorded, not forced). Comparable columns are per-user decode speed and TTFT; AA-style, not AA-leaderboard-comparable (synthetic prompts, model-tokenizer units, one batch vs their 72-h medians).

Table format everywhere: primary cells are TPOT p50 ms (aggregate tok/s); AA cells are per-user decode p50 tok/s (TTFT p50 ms). Cross-window TPOT noise floor is ±0.2% (re-run arms reproduce to ±0.02 ms). Provider numbers are matched on workload shape but carry live multi-tenant load — same-ballpark placement, not a ranking.

How to read the highlights. One rule, applied to every table: the green fill marks the best cell in the row, and any cell within 1% of it is marked too (that is measurement noise, so co-leaders are shown as co-leaders). Every data row therefore carries at least one green. The amber left bar is a separate, composable mark: the cell misses the stated threshold for that table — for agentic tables, p95 TTFT above the 1-second interactivity SLO. A cell can be both (fastest arm, still over the SLO). Amber text with a † means the cell is not a valid comparison (cyankiwi's runaway generations); those cells are excluded from the row ranking. Tinted rows are baselines or our own arms, as noted per table. Two tables are structurally not row-wise comparisons — the kernel-stage narrative and the provider ranking — and each states its own convention underneath.
