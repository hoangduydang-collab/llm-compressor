Method

What was measured

aiperf 0.8.0 drives synthetic traffic against each arm's OpenAI endpoint; engine held fixed (vLLM 0.24.0, identical flags, FP8 KV cache). Quant arms: one 8×H100 node (TP8). BF16: two nodes (TP16/ray). Two workloads:

  • Reasoning — 1,000 input tokens, 8k forced decode (ignore_eos; isolates kernel speed from model verbosity); temp 0.6, thinking on; concurrency 1/4/16/64.
  • Agentic — multi-turn with a growing, mostly-cacheable prefix; shape calibrated on M3 itself with tau2-bench (7,300-token cacheable prefix, 14±3 turns, ~100 output tokens/turn). warm = sticky sessions (KV reuse); cold = cache-defeated control.

Current headline numbers are from the Humming-kernel windows (20260725T122256Z, 20260726T033158Z, GPTQ checkpoint; the Humming serve attests the patched kernel fail-closed before every benchmark). The five-arm checkpoint comparison is from 07-22 and lives in history. Arm-vs-arm claims are valid; the provider comparison is matched on workload shape (single request, 1k prompt, decode rate after first token) but providers carry live multi-tenant load — same-ballpark placement, not a ranking.

Warm holds a 1-second p95 SLO to concurrency 16 and misses at 32 (1,289 ms) — BF16 held 32 in its window, on double the hardware. Two findings:

  • Prefix caching is worth 3–5× on TTFT under load, and no arm holds a 1 s SLO past concurrency 1 uncached. Agentic serving without KV reuse is not viable, at any precision.
  • The cache dominates turn depth: warm TTFT falls as conversations deepen (deep turns are almost entirely cached prefix). vLLM 0.24 never reports cached_tokens; caching was verified by a preflight double-prompt probe plus the warm-vs-cold delta.
