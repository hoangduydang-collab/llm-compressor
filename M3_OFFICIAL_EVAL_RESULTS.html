Which M3 quants are safe to ship — and why AWQ isn't yet

Two paired evaluations of our in-house quants against the BF16 baseline. GPTQ and MXFP8 recover the baseline cleanly. AWQ carries a large, specific regression on reasoning tasks that turns out to be one mechanism — the model stops terminating its own thinking — not broad capability loss.

Pipeline: benchmarks quality.run_ab Runs: full4 (07-20) · tok64k (07-21) · sampling probe (07-22) Engine: vLLM 0.24.0 · lm-eval 0.4.10 Baseline: MiniMax-M3 BF16

Bottom line

Read this if you have thirty seconds

Across seven tasks, GPTQ (W4A8) and MXFP8 are ship-grade — every task recovers ≥97.4% of BF16, token spend matches the baseline, and the handful of answer changes are symmetric noise. In-house AWQ (W4A8, recipe r5) is not shippable as-is: it loses 30 points on GPQA and 6 on IFEval. But the damage is not "AWQ can't reason" — when it produces an answer, its accuracy matches BF16. The loss is that on hard prompts it falls into runaway thinking loops, exhausts the generation budget, and emits nothing, which scores as zero. Doubling the budget to 64k did not fix it — but switching from greedy to MiniMax's recommended sampling recovers roughly half the stuck items, so part of the gap is a decoding artifact, not pure recipe damage. A community AWQ quant is worse than ours on every axis, so this is an AWQ-on-M3 problem, not a flaw unique to our recipe.

Method

What was measured, and what the numbers do and don't mean

Both runs use the official benchmarks A/B pipeline: each quant is served on its own node and scored against a shared BF16 endpoint. Two complementary runs:

  • full4 — breadth. Four arms (BF16, GPTQ, MXFP8, AWQ) × seven tasks, 32k-token budget.
  • tok64k — depth. Five arms (adds a community AWQ, cyankiwi W4A16) × the two most-damaged tasks (GPQA-Diamond, IFEval), 64k-token budget, with per-request token accounting.

Decoding is greedy (temperature 0), the suite's reproducibility convention. Thinking is on (M3 is a reasoning model; the minimax_m3 parser strips the <think> block and scores only the final answer — which is why an exhausted, answer-less response scores zero). GPQA-Diamond is graduate-level science with long chain-of-thought; IFEval is verifiable instruction-following, but on a reasoning model it too runs through a full think phase before answering.

Comparability caveat

These scores are valid for quant-vs-quant and quant-vs-BF16 decisions — all arms run the identical protocol. They are not directly comparable to public leaderboards: we use greedy decoding (MiniMax's own recipe is temperature 1.0 / top_p 0.95) and a task subset. The greedy choice matters for the AWQ finding and is the subject of an open follow-up (below).

Root cause

One mechanism: reasoning that never terminates

The GPQA and IFEval regressions look like two problems but are one. On hard prompts the AWQ model enters its <think> phase and never converges — it burns the entire budget mid-thought and returns an empty answer. Three independent lines of evidence:

1 · Extra budget does not rescue it

Of the 74 GPQA items AWQ exhausted at a 32k budget, 63 were still exhausted at 64k — only 11 recovered, and 14 new ones broke. Accuracy moved 0.571 → 0.576 (flat). This is non-termination, not "needs more room." AWQ's mean GPQA completion length is 26,463 tokens vs BF16's 12,059.

2 · IFEval failure is 100% empties, not lost instruction-following

When AWQ actually answers an IFEval prompt, it passes at 0.892 — statistically identical to BF16's 0.901. The entire −8-point gap is the 9.1% of prompts that exhaust the budget and emit nothing. Counterfactually crediting those empties at BF16's answer-rate reconstructs 0.893 = BF16's score exactly. Instruction-following capability is intact.

3 · The exhausted transcripts are literal loops

Repetition analysis of the exhausted reasoning tails (zlib compression ratio; lower = more repetitive): BF16/GPTQ/MXFP8 sit at ~0.15 — coherent reasoning that simply overran. Our AWQ is 0.078 (semantic loops — re-deriving the same argument). The community AWQ is 0.004 — degenerate single-token spam (drop drop drop…, the the the…).

Recipe vindication

The external cyankiwi AWQ (W4A16) is worse than our r5 on every axis — more spend, more exhaustion (55.6% vs 38.9% on GPQA), lower score (0.455 vs 0.576), more one-sided flips (71/2 vs 50/5). The failure mode is intrinsic to AWQ on M3's long reasoning, and our recipe is the better AWQ of the two.

Follow-up — resolved

Sampling breaks the loops for our AWQ, not for the community quant

Reasoning models are known to loop under greedy decoding (DeepSeek-R1's card warns of it; MiniMax recommends temperature 1.0 / top_p 0.95), and our runs are greedy. A scoped probe re-ran the previously-exhausted GPQA items under both greedy and MiniMax's sampling at a fixed 32k budget. The result splits cleanly by arm — non-termination rate on that stuck-item subset:

Arm · exhausted-item subsetGreedySampledChange
BF16 (control)64.0%62.7%flat
GPTQ (control)52.2%58.0%flat
AWQ r5 (in-house)76.0%49.3%−27 pts
cyankiwi AWQ94.0%87.3%−7 pts

Sampling does not reduce exhaustion on BF16 or GPTQ — confirming their overruns are genuinely hard items, not loops (a clean control that the test isn't just a termination knob). It substantially rescues our AWQ: non-termination drops 76% → 49%, and 74% of the stuck items terminate on at least one sampled attempt (mean 51% of attempts). So a large share of our AWQ regression is a greedy × quant artifact — the true deployment-mode (sampling) damage is smaller than the greedy scores imply, though not zero. The community cyankiwi quant barely moves (94% → 87%; only 30% of items ever terminate): its degenerate single-token loops are robust to sampling and represent genuine, severe damage.

What this means for the verdict

The paired verdicts stand (greedy is the fixed, shared protocol), but the AWQ regression now splits into a greedy-induced component — fixable decode-side (sampling, repetition penalty, or a think-budget cap) — and a residual recipe component. GPQA and IFEval share the one root cause, so a single fix addresses both. Thinking stays on throughout: it is the standard, correct setting for a reasoning model, and disabling it would both break convention and hide a real deployment risk. Full reasoning transcripts for every exhausted request are captured, so the loops can be inspected without a rerun.
